`freshnessLabel` computes `minutes = Math.floor((Date.now() - fetchedAt) / 60_000)` and returns "as of just now" when `minutes < 1`. If `fetchedAt` is in the future relative to the device clock — which happens when the device clock is behind the timestamp source, after a clock/timezone correction, or when a cached `fetchedAt` was written on a device whose clock later moved backward — `minutes` is negative, still satisfies `minutes < 1`, and the UI confidently labels arbitrarily old cached data as freshly fetched. This is distinct from the tracked NaN and never-advancing freshness issues.

**Fix:** clamp elapsed time at zero (`Math.max(0, Math.floor(...))`) or explicitly treat `Date.now() < fetchedAt` as an anomaly, so a skewed-future timestamp doesn't masquerade as just fetched.
